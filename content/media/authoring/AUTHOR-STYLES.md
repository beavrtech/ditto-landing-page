# The Scope — per-author byline styles

The Scope is one masthead with several correspondents, not five blogs sharing a URL. Every article obeys [TONE-OF-VOICE.md](../TONE-OF-VOICE.md) in full: the non-negotiables, the anti-slop standard, the mechanics, the anatomy. Nothing in this file overrides any of it.

What varies by author is the **journalistic role**: the vantage point the piece is reported from, the sentence rhythm, and one or two signature moves. The test: a regular reader should be able to guess the byline from the body copy, and an editor should not be able to tell the article apart from the house style checklist.

Each author's register is derived from how they actually write and talk day to day, so drafted or assisted articles still sound like the person who signs them.

## How to apply

After loading the house rules, load the block for the article's byline and apply it as a second, thinner layer: role, rhythm, signature moves, off-limits. When a rule here seems to conflict with TONE-OF-VOICE.md, TONE-OF-VOICE.md wins.

Picking the byline when the user has not named one: match the shape to the role and **propose, never assume**. Explainer → Alexis. Opinion or analysis → Pierre Poirmeur. Client case story → Pierre Masse. Decision guide or Q&A → Tristan. Commercial rules and tenders → Ugo. The author must exist in `content/media/authors/` (real people only).

## Alexis de Taillac — the specialist correspondent

Head of Compliance. He writes structured, exhaustive notes, lists the questions before the answers, and flags ambiguity explicitly. Methodical first, playful second.

- **Role:** the desk expert who has read the standard so the reader doesn't have to. Owns the foundational explainers (ISO, management systems, framework mechanics).
- **Rhythm:** medium sentences, tight paragraphs, generous structure. H3s, numbered steps, and comparison tables come naturally to him; he is the author most allowed to use them.
- **Signature moves:** anticipates the reader's next question and answers it in the following paragraph. Draws precise boundaries ("this applies after policies are written and before submission"). His one vivid turn per piece is dry, almost deadpan.
- **Off-limits:** hype, motivational closes. Alexis never says something is exciting; he shows why it is consequential.

## Pierre Poirmeur — the columnist

Co-founder and CEO. He argues in distilled one-liners, one claim per sentence, no hedging, and calls a failed negotiation a failed negotiation.

- **Role:** the opinion column. Occasional pieces, each built on one thesis the reader could disagree with, argued from first principles and a real situation (including ones that went wrong).
- **Rhythm:** short declarative sentences. Paragraphs of two or three lines. The thesis appears in the standfirst and again, sharpened, in the close.
- **Signature moves:** names the underlying principle behind a messy situation ("a discount without a counterpart is not a negotiation"). Uses his own missteps as evidence. Comfortable ending on a verdict.
- **Off-limits:** surveys of viewpoints, "on the other hand" padding, product mentions. A Poirmeur piece that hedges is a failed piece.

## Pierre Masse — the field reporter

QHSE, CSR and supply chain expert working directly with customers. He writes with real energy, but switches to clean factual sequences when reporting: what happened, in order, with the outcome.

- **Role:** reportage from the ground. Articles anchored in a real client situation (anonymized as needed): what the company faced, what was tried, what happened, what to copy.
- **Rhythm:** narrative paragraphs alternating with terse factual beats. His energy shows up as momentum and concrete detail, never as exclamation marks or emoji (banned house-wide anyway).
- **Signature moves:** the before/after fact pair ("Platinum in 2023, Bronze by mid-2026"). Credits the practitioners in the story for what worked. "Good to know:" asides carry his field tricks.
- **Off-limits:** abstraction. If a Masse paragraph has no company, person, number, or deadline in it, it gets cut or reassigned.

## Tristan Salaun — the explanatory interviewer

Account Executive, and the most conversational writer of the team: full sentences, context for people out of the loop, clarifying questions before conclusions, options weighed honestly.

- **Role:** the reader-advocate. Q&A-shaped pieces and decision guides that start from the question a practitioner actually asked and walk to a pragmatic recommendation.
- **Rhythm:** flowing, courteous, slightly longer sentences than the house average, still one idea each. Transitions do real work ("to be clear about what can be shared and what cannot").
- **Signature moves:** states the reader's situation back to them before answering. Distinguishes the simple option from the advanced one, honestly, with the trade-off. His FAQ sections are the best in the house because he genuinely collects the questions.
- **Off-limits:** verdict-first bluntness. Tristan earns the recommendation; he does not open with it as a provocation. (The standfirst still answers, per house rules; his body then shows the weighing.)

## Ugo Le Borgne — the practitioner's notebook

Account Executive. He thinks in maxims ("never negotiate by email", "trust your client"), moves fast, and values the relationship over the paperwork.

- **Role:** rules-from-the-trenches pieces on commercial reality: tenders, buyer requirements, what clients actually respond to. Natural owner of the ISO-in-tenders beat.
- **Rhythm:** the shortest sentences on the masthead. Numbered rules or steps, each rule followed by the deal or the conversation that taught it.
- **Signature moves:** the earned maxim: a plain-language rule the reader can repeat in a meeting, immediately backed by a specific story. Skeptical of paper guarantees, insistent on relationships and timing.
- **Off-limits:** theory without a story, and swagger without evidence. Every maxim must have its receipt. House rules already ban the emoji and exclamation marks; the confidence stays, the decoration goes.

## Editor's notes

- Invited authors (`invited: true` in their author file, like Claire Sikorav) have no house register on purpose. They write as themselves; editing aligns a guest piece with the non-negotiables and mechanics only. Correct facts, mechanics, and slop; preserve voice. A guest piece that sounds like the masthead has been over-edited.

- One byline per article (house rule). These are registers, not templates: if a draft ticks every box above but reads generic, the register has failed. Rewrite from the author's vantage point instead of adding more signature moves.
- An author writing outside their home role is fine (Alexis can write a how-to); the register travels with the person, not the shape.
- Revisit each block once an author has 3 or 4 published pieces: replace the derived cues with quotes from their published Scope work.
